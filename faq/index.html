Watt-hours per million output tokens (Wh/M)

What does Wh/M mean?

Watt-hours per million output tokens: the GPU energy a serving configuration uses to generate a million output tokens. Full answer

How do I convert joules per token to watt-hours per million tokens?

Multiply joules per token by 1,000,000 and divide by 3,600. The reference row's 0.2285 J per token becomes 63.5 Wh/M. Full answer

Is Wh per million tokens the same as kWh per billion tokens?

Yes, and the same as MWh per trillion tokens; the three scale together. Full answer

Does the unit include the prompt?

The energy includes prompt processing, because the benchmark divides steady-state GPU energy by the output tokens generated in the window; the denominator counts output tokens only. Full answer

Energy per token

What is energy per token?

GPU energy divided by the output tokens generated in the same steady-state window, in joules per output token. Full answer

How is energy per token measured?

By reading the GPU's energy counter (through NVML or DCGM; the leaderboard uses Zeus) at the start and end of a steady-state window under a fixed load, and dividing the difference by the output tokens generated in that window. Full answer

Why do estimates of energy per token differ so much from measurements?

Estimates multiply a rated power by an assumed utilisation and divide by an assumed throughput, usually a single request at a time. Full answer

Does energy per token include the host, the network and cooling?

Not on this index. The figures are GPU energy only. A whole-system reading adds the host, memory, network and fans (about 1.79× for a DGX B200, from NVIDIA's rated system and GPU power), and the facility's PUE adds cooling and distribution... Full answer

Running batch

What is running batch?

The average number of requests a serving engine was decoding at once over the measurement window (vLLM's num_requests_running). Full answer

Why does an idle GPU use more energy per token than a busy one?

Because a GPU's power does not fall in step with its work. On the index, Qwen 3 32B on 2 × H100 draws 850 W serving 8 requests at once and 1,178 W serving 256, 39 percent more power for 9.9× the tokens per second. Full answer

What is the difference between running batch and batch size?

Batch size is usually a configured limit (the cap, vLLM's max_num_seqs) or a fixed count in an offline benchmark. Full answer

What running batch should I ask a vendor to measure at?

The one you expect in production, stated as a number, with the median and p99 inter-token latency your users will accept. Full answer

Floor

What does the index mean by a floor?

The lowest GPU energy per output token measured for a model and GPU among configurations at or under a 500 ms median inter-token latency, at high concurrency. Full answer

Is the floor what my deployment will use?

No. It is the best measured configuration on a busy server, GPU only. Full answer

What should I do with a vendor figure below the floor?

Ask for the measurement: the running batch held, the latency over the window, the method, the tool and the logs. Full answer

Does the floor change if the latency ceiling changes?

It can. At 500 ms, the leaderboard's default, no measured configuration is excluded. Full answer

4 questions

GPU-only boundary

What does GPU-only mean on the index?

That the energy was read from the GPU devices alone over a steady-state window. Full answer

How do I take a GPU-only figure to the facility meter?

Multiply by the system-to-GPU power ratio (1.79 for a DGX B200: 14.3 kW rated against 8.0 kW of GPUs) and by the site's PUE. Full answer

Why not publish whole-system figures?

Because they cannot be compared across vendors or sites: the host, the fans and the cooling design differ with every server and building, while the GPU figure for a named model on a named card at a stated load can be measured anywhere. Full answer

Does the boundary include the energy of processing the prompt?

Yes. The benchmark divides steady-state GPU energy, which includes prefill, by the output tokens generated; the prompt's tokens are not in the denominator. Full answer

What is location-based grid carbon intensity?

The average carbon intensity of the electricity generated where the load runs, in gCO2e per kWh, before contracts or certificates. Full answer

What is the difference between location-based and market-based?

Location-based uses the average intensity of the grid where the electricity was consumed. Full answer

Can I file Ember's figure in my Scope 2 report?

Use it for comparison, not as the filed factor. It is a lifecycle, production-based figure on net generation, not a Scope 2 combustion factor, and it is an annual average that a round-the-clock GPU load does not follow hour by hour. Full answer

A median inter-token latency of at most 500 ms, the ML.ENERGY Leaderboard's default for every LLM task. Full answer

What is inter-token latency?

The time between one output token and the next for a single request while the model is generating, usually reported as a median and a p99 over a window. Full answer

Why state a latency ceiling with an energy figure at all?

Power usage effectiveness (PUE)

What is PUE?

Power usage effectiveness: a data centre's total facility energy divided by the energy delivered to its IT equipment, per ISO/IEC 30134-2. Full answer

How do I use PUE with watt-hours per million tokens?

Multiply the GPU figure by the system-to-GPU power ratio and by the PUE. Full answer

Does a low PUE mean efficient AI inference?

No. PUE measures the building's overhead, not the work the GPUs did. Full answer

Which regulation asks for PUE?

In the EU, the Energy Efficiency Directive (EU) 2023/1791, Article 12, with Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2024/1364: data centres with an installed IT power demand of at least 500 kW report facility indicators including PUE. Full answer
