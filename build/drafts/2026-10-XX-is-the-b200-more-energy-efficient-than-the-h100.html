It uses less energy per token because it produces more tokens in every second of that draw. The distinction matters to the facility. An eight-GPU {{SYS_NAME}} is rated at about {{SYS_KW}} kW, {{SYS_RATIO}}× what its cards draw, so a B200 fleet is a bigger feed that produces more tokens per kilowatt-hour, and the envelope decides how many boxes you can stand up before the tokens do. Power sizes the feed. Energy per token tells you what the feed produced.

The second lever

Here is the part I would put in the memo. The widest generation gap on the table is {{PAIR_MAX_RATIO}}×. The same model on the same H100 cards moves {{L32_RATIO}}× in energy per token between a running batch of {{L32_FIRST_BATCH}} and {{L32_BEST_BATCH}}, and that lever costs nothing in hardware. A department that serves a handful of people at a time and buys B200s has bought a faster car for a road where it never gets out of second gear. In the deployments I see, that is the usual order of events: the card gets upgraded, the utilisation does not, and the energy per token barely moves. Consolidate first. Then the generation change is worth its median, and the new card carries the load on fewer cards, which is also fewer boxes to power and cool.

What I am not telling you

Two cards, one benchmark. The leaderboard ran {{GPU_LIST}} and nothing else, so there is nothing here about the H200, the GB200 or anyone else's silicon. On the index, {{H100_ONLY_WORD}} models have an H100 row and no B200 row ({{H100_ONLY_LIST}}), and {{B200_ONLY_WORD}} have the reverse ({{B200_ONLY_LIST}}), so the {{PAIR_COUNT}} pairs are the models that happen to have both. A TDP is a rating, not a reading; the measured draws above are the ones to plan on. And every row is a floor at high load: your fleet sits above it on both cards, and the question is by how much on each.

What to do with it

  • Ask for both cards at your running batch and your latency. The pairs above can be re-cut at a matched cap or a matched running batch from the API; ask the vendor to do the same with the configuration they are quoting, and refuse a ratio that comes without its two running batches.
  • Count cards, not only watts. On {{PAIR_FEWER_WORD}} of the {{PAIR_COUNT}} pairs the B200 holds the model on fewer cards. Fewer cards is fewer boxes, and that saving sits outside the GPU-only figure on this site; the method takes a figure to the meter.
  • Size the feed on the rating, judge the kit on the energy. A rating of {{B200_TDP_LABEL}} per card is what the electrical engineer plans for; {{B200_AVG_W}} W per card is what the benchmark saw on these rows; watt-hours per million tokens is what you are buying.
  • Fix the utilisation before the purchase order. The concurrency ladder is the bigger lever and the cheaper one.
