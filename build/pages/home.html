The energy behind the token

Lead Green is a public index of LLM inference energy per token: the GPU watt-hours it takes to serve a million output tokens, by model, GPU and configuration, with the grid carbon to price it anywhere. Every figure is from a named public measurement or a manufacturer's specification, dated and linked. The data is open, there is a JSON API and an MCP server, and anyone with a GPU will be able to add a measurement.

Unit watt-hours per million output tokens (Wh/M). One Wh/M is 0.0036 joules per token. GPU only, busy server. Every row is a floor. Updated {{UPDATED_LONG}}.

Why it exists

Everyone buys tokens. Nobody is told the energy.

Enterprises are buying GPUs by the rack and tokens by the billion, and neither invoice carries an energy figure. Model vendors have published, at most, a per-prompt average of energy, carbon and water with no load, output length or method a buyer could compare (Google, August 2025; Mistral, July 2025). None publishes watt-hours per million output tokens at a stated concurrency. The cloud providers do not itemise one. The measurements that do exist are academic and scattered across leaderboards and papers, in different units, on different hardware, under different load. A CIO who wants to know what a million tokens costs in watt-hours has nowhere to look it up.

Meanwhile the questions are arriving. Boards want the AI line in the energy report. Auditors want to know how Scope 2, or Scope 3 for a hosted API, grows when the assistants roll out. The EU AI Act already requires providers of general-purpose AI models to document the model's energy consumption for the AI Office, with exemptions for open-weight models. No law asks anyone for serving energy per token; buyers should. Sovereign and on-premise deployments run inside a fixed power envelope, so energy per token sets how much AI a site can serve at all.

Lead Green puts the public measurements in one unit on one page, explains what they mean, exposes them to software, and adds measurements of its own as they are made. It is run and paid for by Katonic, which sells a sovereign AI platform and benefits when buyers ask the questions on this site. It takes no money from hardware, cloud or model vendors.

Who it is for

Five desks, one number

CIO and CTO

To size a GPU estate against a power budget, to know what on-premise inference would cost in energy before a hosted invoice becomes a power bill, and to answer the board. Hosted APIs cannot be measured from outside; the why page says what to ask the vendor instead.

Sustainability officer

To put AI serving into the energy and Scope 2 lines with a defensible method, and to know which questions to send back to the vendor.

Platform engineer

To see what concurrency, quantisation and GPU generation do to energy per token, and to measure their own cluster against a public baseline.

Procurement

To ask every bidder for the same figure at the same load, in a clause that names the unit and the latency limit and tells you what to fill in, with a scoring method beside it.

Facility and capacity planner

You own the feed, the power envelope and the capacity meeting. The index gives you GPU energy per unit of work and the two multipliers that take it to the meter, so a contracted feed becomes tokens per hour at a stated PUE and server overhead. It does not give you PUE, water, waste heat or anything else in facility units.

How to use it

Three ways in

  1. Read. Start with the three tiles and the concurrency section of the index. If you take one thing away, take the {{LADDER_32_RATIO}}× ladder.
  2. Compute. Pull index.json and grid.json from the API. Multiply Wh per million by your monthly tokens for energy; multiply by your grid's gCO2e per kWh and divide by 1,000 for kg CO2e per billion tokens.
  3. Ask. Add the MCP server to your assistant and ask in plain words. It answers with the row, the source and the caveat.
