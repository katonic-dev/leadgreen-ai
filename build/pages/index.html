The GPU energy it takes to serve a million tokens, by model, GPU and configuration. Every figure is from a public measurement or a manufacturer's specification, and every source is listed at the foot of the page. Updated {{UPDATED_LONG}}.

Green here is a direction, not a verdict. This table ranks serving configurations by GPU energy per token and by nothing else.

Unit watt-hours per million output tokens (Wh/M). One Wh/M is 0.0036 joules per token. GPU only, busy server. Every row is a floor. The same data as JSON and through the MCP server.

Concurrency

The idle server is the expensive one

Energy per token depends on how many requests the server is handling at once. Below is the same Qwen 3 32B on the same two H100 cards, all {{L32_COUNT_WORD}} measured configurations, from a running batch of {{L32_FIRST_BATCH}} to the index row at {{L32_BEST_BATCH}}. Energy per token falls {{L32_RATIO}}×, from {{L32_FIRST}} to {{L32_BEST}} Wh/M, while median inter-token latency stays at {{L32_MAX_ITL}} ms or under. Power rises {{L32_POWER_PCT}} percent, from {{L32_FIRST_W}} W to {{L32_BEST_W}} W, while throughput rises {{L32_TPS_RATIO}}×, from {{L32_FIRST_TPS}} to {{L32_BEST_TPS}} tokens per second. Energy per token falls because the second effect dwarfs the first. Past the best row the curve turns: at a cap of {{L32_TURN_CAP}} the same pair of cards reads {{L32_TURN_WH}} Wh/M at {{L32_TURN_ITL}} ms.

Carbon

Same tokens, different grid

Energy per token does not change with geography. Carbon per token does. This table prices the reference row ({{REF_MODEL}}, {{REF_GPUS}}, {{REF_WH_1}} Wh/M) against each grid's average emissions intensity for {{EMBER_YEAR}}. A billion output tokens on the Saudi grid carries about {{REF_KG_SAU}} kg CO2e of GPU-side emissions. The same billion tokens on the Norwegian grid carries {{REF_KG_NOR}} kg.

{{CARBON_ROWS}}
Grid emissions intensity, {{EMBER_YEAR}} annual average, location-based, and the reference row priced against it
Grid g CO2e per kWh kg CO2e per billion output tokens

Which grids. The {{LISTED_GRID_COUNT}} grids are {{MARKET_GRID_COUNT}} markets Katonic sells in or expects to, plus {{REFERENCE_GRID_WORD}} reference grids. Ember's {{EMBER_YEAR}} figures run from {{GRID_MIN_NAME}} at {{GRID_MIN_G}} to {{GRID_MAX_NAME}} at {{GRID_MAX_G}} gCO2e per kWh, a spread of {{GRID_SPREAD}}×; all {{GRID_COUNT}} areas plus World are in grid.json. Area names are shortened from Ember's labels.

What the figure is. Grid figures are Ember's yearly emissions intensity (CC BY 4.0), September 2026 release. {{EMBER_BASIS}} The table is location-based in the ESRS E1-6 sense, so what it prices belongs in the Scope 2 location-based line of E1-6, not in the E1-5 energy line. The other method, market-based, nets the buyer's contracted supply (certificates and power purchase agreements) and needs the buyer's own contracts.

Why {{EMBER_YEAR}}. It is the latest year with a value for every grid listed. {{GRID_2025_COUNT}} of Ember's {{GRID_COUNT}} areas already have a 2025 value, but {{GRID_NO_2025}} do not yet; grid.json carries 2023 to 2025 per area where present, with the latest year named. A flat load. A GPU server draws much the same power around the clock, while an annual average weights each hour by how much was generated in it, so on a grid whose clean supply peaks at midday a flat load sees a higher figure than the average, and on one with steady clean supply much the same. Hourly intensity, contracted supply, on-site generation and facility overhead are not counted here. Multiply any row of the index by the grid figure and divide by 1,000 to price a different model.

Per query

Batch size one, a different question

The AI Energy Score measures something else: GPU watt-hours for 1,000 text-generation queries, one query at a time, on NVIDIA H100 80GB GPUs: one card for class A and B models, several cards for class C. A query is one prompt from the benchmark's 1,000-prompt text-generation set, run at batch size 1 with the response fixed at {{AIES_FIXED_TOKENS}} generated tokens for non-reasoning models, averaged over ten passes. It is a controlled probe, not a serving workload, so its watt-hours are not comparable with the index and are not converted. It is shown because the ranking within a class is useful.

Method

What is counted, and what is not

  • The unit. Watt-hours per million output tokens equals joules per output token multiplied by one million and divided by 3,600. The reference row, {{REF_J}} J per token, becomes {{REF_WH_1}} Wh/M.
  • Counted. GPU energy only, read from the device by the benchmark's measurement library (Zeus) at steady state, with vLLM serving the model on a real H100 80GB HBM3 or B200 server. Prompt processing is included, because the benchmark divides steady-state GPU energy by the output tokens generated in the window (its steady_state_energy_per_token); energy per request is that figure times the average output length.
  • Not counted. Host CPU and memory, network and storage, cooling and the rest of the facility, time the GPU spends idle between requests, and the carbon embodied in the hardware. For scale on that last item, NVIDIA's own cradle-to-gate footprint for one HGX B200 baseboard, eight GPUs, is {{B200_EMBODIED_KG}} kg CO2e.
  • Water. The index measures electricity, not water. For a public scale, Google puts the median Gemini Apps text prompt at {{GOOGLE_ML}} mL (August 2025) and Mistral puts a {{MISTRAL_TOKENS}}-token reply at {{MISTRAL_ML}} mL (July 2025). Neither is per million tokens at a stated load, and site water depends on the cooling design and the climate.
  • To the meter. Two multipliers take a GPU figure to the facility meter, and both are the operator's own. The system-to-GPU power ratio covers the host, memory, network and fans: a {{SYS_NAME}} is rated at {{SYS_KW}} kW against {{SYS_GPU_KW}} kW for its {{SYS_GPU_COUNT}} GPUs, a ratio of {{SYS_RATIO}}. Power usage effectiveness (PUE, per ISO/IEC 30134-2) covers cooling and the rest of the building, and only the operator knows theirs. As an illustration, with that ratio and an assumed PUE of {{PUE_EXAMPLE}}, both assumptions and not measurements: {{REF_WH_1}} × {{SYS_RATIO}} × {{PUE_EXAMPLE}} = {{METER_WH}} Wh/M at the meter, about {{METER_TOKENS_B}} billion tokens per facility MWh. For a public scale, Google's August 2025 figures put the accelerators at {{GOOGLE_ACCEL_PCT}} percent of {{GOOGLE_WH}} Wh per median text prompt and the accelerator-only boundary at {{GOOGLE_NARROW_WH}} Wh, so its full stack is {{GOOGLE_STACK_LO}} to {{GOOGLE_STACK_HI}} times the accelerator figure.
  • Not measurable. Closed-weight models served over an API cannot be measured from outside the operator, and this index says nothing about them.
  • The reference row. {{REF_MODEL}} on {{REF_GPUS}} is the row the carbon table and the worked examples are priced on because it is a dense model, on two cards, with a full ladder of {{L32_COUNT_WORD}} measured configurations; it ranks {{REF_RANK}} of {{ROW_COUNT}}. It is a scaling point, not a claim about typical deployments.
  • A floor. Every index row is the best configuration found under a latency limit, at high concurrency. A real deployment sits above it, often far above it. The concurrency section shows how far.
  • The latency ceiling. {{ITL}} ms is the leaderboard's own default for every LLM task, chosen to match it. No measured configuration reaches it, so it changes no row. Tighter ceilings would: {{SENS_SENTENCE}} None of these is applied to the index.
  • Workload. The index uses the Text Conversation task. The same leaderboard's Problem Solving task costs {{GPQA_RATIO_MIN}} to {{GPQA_RATIO_MAX}} times the energy per response for the same model and GPU, because its answers run {{GPQA_OUT_MIN}} to {{GPQA_OUT_MAX}} tokens against {{CHAT_OUT_MIN}} to {{CHAT_OUT_MAX}}, {{GPQA_TOK_SENTENCE}} (table). Energy per token is the fairer comparison across workloads; energy per response is what a bill sees.
  • Grid intensity. Annual generation average per country, in grams of CO2e per kWh, from Ember. Hourly values, contracted supply and self-generation are not reflected.
  • Words. The words-per-token conversion uses the common English rule of thumb of about three quarters of a word per token. It is a rough guide, nothing more.

Own measurements

None yet

Lead Green has not published a measurement of its own. The first will be Qwen 3 14B in BF16 on one H100: the whole card, then the same card split into two 3g.40gb slices, each serving its own BF16 copy under the same load at the same time, with GPU power sampled through NVIDIA DCGM on a server Katonic operates. It is like-for-like on precision and will sit beside the public 1 × H100 BF16 rows for the same model ({{Q14_FIRST_WH}} Wh/M at a cap of {{Q14_FIRST_CAP}} down to {{Q14_LAST_WH}} at a cap of {{Q14_LAST_CAP}}). The second will be Qwen 3 32B: the whole card in BF16, then two 3g.40gb slices each serving an FP8 or INT4 copy under the same load. It is not like-for-like on precision and will be labelled so, beside the public 1 × H100 rows ({{Q32_1GPU_WH_LIST}} Wh/M). MIG exposes no per-slice power and NCCL does not run across slices, so both read the card's energy as a whole with every slice loaded. It will be labelled as ours, with hardware, driver, serving engine and prompt set named, and it will sit beside the public figures rather than in place of them. The protocol it will follow is on the contribute page, and anyone with a GPU can follow it too.
