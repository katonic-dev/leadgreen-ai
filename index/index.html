Green Tokens

The GPU energy it takes to serve a million tokens, by model, GPU and configuration. Every figure is from a public measurement or a manufacturer's specification, and every source is listed at the foot of the page. Updated 18 October 2026.

Green here is a direction, not a verdict. This table ranks serving configurations by GPU energy per token and by nothing else.

Unit watt-hours per million output tokens (Wh/M). One Wh/M is 0.0036 joules per token. GPU only, busy server. Every row is a floor. The same data as JSON and through the MCP server.

Two generations

The same model on H100 and B200

14 models were measured on both GPUs. The newer card's advantage runs from 4.55× on Qwen 3 235B A22B Instruct to 1.05× on NVIDIA Nemotron Nano 9B V2. The gap also depends on which configurations were swept. The 4.55× pair compares an H100 row that was run only at eight cards and a cap of at most 256 requests with a B200 row at a running batch of 3,765 on four cards; at the same cap of 256 the B200 row reads 197.5 Wh/M against the H100's 303.5, a gap of 1.54×. The two Nemotron pairs compare runs whose average answers are 3.2 and 2.4 times longer on the B200 (462 and 510 tokens on the H100 against 1,464 and 1,242).

A card that draws more power can still use less energy per token, because it finishes more tokens per second. Power tells you the size of the feed. Energy per token tells you what the feed produced. The pairs, with card counts and running batches, are on the H100 vs B200 page.

Concurrency

The idle server is the expensive one

Energy per token depends on how many requests the server is handling at once. Below is the same Qwen 3 32B on the same two H100 cards, all nine measured configurations, from a running batch of 8 to the index row at 256. Energy per token falls 7.1×, from 452 to 63.5 Wh/M, while median inter-token latency stays at 44 ms or under. Power rises 39 percent, from 850 W to 1,178 W, while throughput rises 9.9×, from 522 to 5,155 tokens per second. Energy per token falls because the second effect dwarfs the first. Past the best row the curve turns: at a cap of 384 the same pair of cards reads 64.6 Wh/M at 55 ms.

Two tasks

Long answers also shrink the batch

The same leaderboard also runs a Problem Solving task, whose answers are far longer. For the 11 model and GPU pairs measured on both tasks, Problem Solving costs 3 to 41 times the energy per response of Text Conversation, because its answers run 5,361 to 8,969 tokens against 462 to 1,464, and because the long outputs cut the batch the server can hold, so energy per token itself is 2.2 to 3.3 times the Text Conversation figure for the Qwen and DeepSeek pairs, and 0.7 to 1.0 times it for the four Nemotron pairs.

Same tokens, different grid

Which grids. The 22 grids are 15 markets Katonic sells in or expects to, plus seven reference grids. Ember's 2024 figures run from Ethiopia at 23.5 to Turkmenistan at 1,306.2 gCO2e per kWh, a spread of 55.6×; all 193 areas plus World are in grid.json, and each listed grid has its own page with the whole index priced on it. Area names are shortened from Ember's labels.

What the figure is. Grid figures are Ember's yearly emissions intensity (CC BY 4.0), October 2026 release. Lifecycle emissions intensity: CO2e per kWh of net generation, built from per-technology lifecycle factors (IPCC AR5 WG3 Annex III midpoints, all gases at GWP-100), so upstream methane, supply chains and plant manufacture are included. Production-based: imports and grid losses are excluded. A location-based figure for comparing places, not a Scope 2 combustion factor to file as reported. The table is location-based in the ESRS E1-6 sense, so what it prices belongs in the Scope 2 location-based line of E1-6, not in the E1-5 energy line. The other method, market-based, nets the buyer's contracted supply (certificates and power purchase agreements) and needs the buyer's own contracts.

Why 2024. It is the latest year with a value for every grid listed. 95 of Ember's 193 areas already have a 2025 value, but Hong Kong do not yet; grid.json carries 2023 to 2025 per area where present, with the latest year named. A flat load. A GPU server draws much the same power around the clock, while an annual average weights each hour by how much was generated in it, so on a grid whose clean supply peaks at midday a flat load sees a higher figure than the average, and on one with steady clean supply much the same. Hourly intensity, contracted supply, on-site generation and facility overhead are not counted here. Multiply any row of the index by the grid figure and divide by 1,000 to price a different model.

Per query

Batch size one, a different question

The AI Energy Score measures something else: GPU watt-hours for 1,000 text-generation queries, one query at a time, on NVIDIA H100 80GB GPUs: one card for class A and B models, several cards for class C. A query is one prompt from the benchmark's 1,000-prompt text-generation set, run at batch size 1 with the response fixed at ten generated tokens for non-reasoning models, averaged over ten passes. It is a controlled probe, not a serving workload, so its watt-hours are not comparable with the index and are not converted. It is shown because the ranking within a class is useful.

Method

What is counted, and what is not

  • The unit. Watt-hours per million output tokens equals joules per output token multiplied by one million and divided by 3,600. The reference row, 0.2285 J per token, becomes 63.5 Wh/M.
  • Counted. GPU energy only, read from the device by the benchmark's measurement library (Zeus) at steady state, with vLLM serving the model on a real H100 80GB HBM3 or B200 server. Prompt processing is included, because the benchmark divides steady-state GPU energy by the output tokens generated in the window (its steady_state_energy_per_token); energy per request is that figure times the average output length.
  • Not counted. Host CPU and memory, network and storage, cooling and the rest of the facility, time the GPU spends idle between requests, and the carbon embodied in the hardware. For scale on that last item, NVIDIA's own cradle-to-gate footprint for one HGX B200 baseboard, eight GPUs, is 2,274 kg CO2e.
  • Water. The index measures electricity, not water. For a public scale, Google puts the median Gemini Apps text prompt at 0.26 mL (August 2025) and Mistral puts a 400-token reply at 45 mL (July 2025). Neither is per million tokens at a stated load, and site water depends on the cooling design and the climate.
  • To the meter. Two multipliers take a GPU figure to the facility meter, and both are the operator's own. The system-to-GPU power ratio covers the host, memory, network and fans: a DGX B200 is rated at 14.3 kW against 8.0 kW for its 8 GPUs, a ratio of 1.79. Power usage effectiveness (PUE, per ISO/IEC 30134-2) covers cooling and the rest of the building, and only the operator knows theirs. As an illustration, with that ratio and an assumed PUE of 1.3, both assumptions and not measurements: 63.5 × 1.79 × 1.3 = 148 Wh/M at the meter, about 6.8 billion tokens per facility MWh. For a public scale, Google's August 2025 figures put the accelerators at 58 percent of 0.24 Wh per median text prompt and the accelerator-only boundary at 0.10 Wh, so its full stack is 1.7 to 2.4 times the accelerator figure.
  • Not measurable. Closed-weight models served over an API cannot be measured from outside the operator, and this index says nothing about them.
  • The reference row. Qwen 3 32B on 2 × H100 is the row the carbon table and the worked examples are priced on because it is a dense model, on two cards, with a full ladder of nine measured configurations; it ranks 19 of 32. It is a scaling point, not a claim about typical deployments.
  • A floor. Every index row is the best configuration found under a latency limit, at high concurrency. A real deployment sits above it, often far above it. The concurrency section shows how far.
  • The latency ceiling. 500 ms is the leaderboard's own default for every LLM task, chosen to match it. No measured configuration reaches it, so it changes no row. Tighter ceilings would: At 250 ms, 1 row changes; at 100 ms, 7 rows change and the lowest becomes Qwen 3 8B on 1 × B200 at 14.7 Wh/M; at 50 ms, 19 rows change and the lowest becomes Qwen 3 8B on 1 × B200 at 17.2 Wh/M and the highest becomes Llama 3.1 405B Instruct FP8 on 8 × H100 at 674.9 Wh/M. None of these is applied to the index.
  • Workload. The index uses the Text Conversation task. The same leaderboard's Problem Solving task costs 3 to 41 times the energy per response for the same model and GPU, because its answers run 5,361 to 8,969 tokens against 462 to 1,464, and because the long outputs cut the batch the server can hold, so energy per token itself is 2.2 to 3.3 times the Text Conversation figure for the Qwen and DeepSeek pairs, and 0.7 to 1.0 times it for the four Nemotron pairs (table). Energy per token is the fairer comparison across workloads; energy per response is what a bill sees.
  • Grid intensity. Annual generation average per country, in grams of CO2e per kWh, from Ember. Hourly values, contracted supply and self-generation are not reflected.
  • Words. The words-per-token conversion uses the common English rule of thumb of about three quarters of a word per token. It is a rough guide, nothing more.

Own measurements

None yet

Lead Green has not published a measurement of its own. The first will be Qwen 3 14B in BF16 on one H100: the whole card, then the same card split into two 3g.40gb slices, each serving its own BF16 copy under the same load at the same time, with GPU power sampled through NVIDIA DCGM on a server Katonic operates. It is like-for-like on precision and will sit beside the public 1 × H100 BF16 rows for the same model (196.1 Wh/M at a cap of 8 down to 31.1 at a cap of 256). The second will be Qwen 3 32B: the whole card in BF16, then two 3g.40gb slices each serving an FP8 or INT4 copy under the same load. It is not like-for-like on precision and will be labelled so, beside the public 1 × H100 rows (479.4, 267.0 and 173.3 Wh/M). MIG exposes no per-slice power and NCCL does not run across slices, so both read the card's energy as a whole with every slice loaded. It will be labelled as ours, with hardware, driver, serving engine and prompt set named, and it will sit beside the public figures rather than in place of them. The protocol it will follow is on the contribute page, and anyone with a GPU can follow it too.

Sources

Every figure, and where it came from

  1. ML.ENERGY Leaderboard, The ML.ENERGY Initiative (started at SymbioticLab, University of Michigan)
    Index, two-generation table, concurrency tables, the workload comparison and the measured GPU power figures. Text Conversation, all 328 stable configurations; Problem Solving, all 187. The leaderboard publishes the runs that passed its stability filter as JSON in its repository; every run, including the unstable ones, is in the gated Hugging Face dataset ml-energy/benchmark-v3. The leaderboard repository the JSON comes from carries no licence file; the benchmark data is published under Apache 2.0 on the dataset card and in the NeurIPS 2025 paper, and the JSON is reproduced here as published measurements on that basis while the ML.ENERGY team is asked to confirm that the licence covers it. Cite: Chung et al., The ML.ENERGY Benchmark: Toward Automated Inference Energy Measurement and Optimization, NeurIPS 2025 Datasets and Benchmarks, arXiv 2505.06371.
    Version 3.0, released 1 December 2025; data compiled 16 February 2026 (the leaderboard's index.json). Retrieved 26 September 2026.
  2. Ember, Yearly electricity data
    Carbon table and the grid API. Emissions intensity of total generation, gCO2e per kWh, 2024. CC BY 4.0.
    October 2026 release. Retrieved 17 October 2026.
  3. NVIDIA Multi-Instance GPU User Guide, Supported MIG Profiles
    MIG profile table.
    Last updated 11 September 2026. Retrieved 18 October 2026.
  4. NVIDIA product specifications: A100, H100, H200, DGX B200
    Maximum TDP and memory in the hardware table; the DGX B200 system power figure used on the "why" page.
    Retrieved 18 October 2026.
  5. NVIDIA, Product Carbon Footprint Summary for NVIDIA HGX B200
    B200 maximum TDP (configurable up to 1,000 W per GPU) and the 2,274 kg CO2e cradle-to-gate figure in the method.
    July 2025. Retrieved 18 October 2026.

    Per-query table. Text generation task, December 2025 round, all 22 rows. Apache-2.0 as tagged on the Space; the project states no separate licence for the results file, which is reproduced as facts with attribution.

    The rough words-per-token conversion in the opening paragraph.
    Retrieved 18 October 2026.
