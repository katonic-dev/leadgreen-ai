Glossary

Location-based grid intensity

Location-based grid intensity is the average carbon intensity of the electricity generated in the area where a GPU runs, in grams of CO2e per kilowatt-hour, before any contracts, certificates or self-generation are considered. The index uses Ember's yearly figures (October 2026 release, 2024, the latest year with a value for every listed grid): 689 gCO2e per kWh for Saudi Arabia, 707 for India, 30 for Norway and 473 for the world. It is a lifecycle figure on net generation, production-based, so upstream methane and plant manufacture are in and imports are out.

The working

How the term is used on the index

Energy per token does not change with geography; carbon per token does. The reference row's 63.5 Wh/M carries 43.7 kg CO2e per billion tokens on Saudi Arabia's grid and 1.9 kg on Norway's, 23× apart for the same tokens on the same GPUs. Across the 193 grids Ember covers the spread is 55.6×, from Ethiopia at 23.5 to Turkmenistan at 1,306.2 gCO2e per kWh.

Location-based is the method ESRS E1-6 and the GHG Protocol pair with market-based, which uses the supply you contracted (certificates, power purchase agreements) and needs your own figures. Ember's number is for comparing places, not for filing as a Scope 2 combustion factor: a GPU server draws much the same power around the clock while an annual average weights each hour by its generation, so on a grid whose clean supply peaks at midday a flat load sees a higher figure than the average. One page per listed grid is under Grids, and every grid is in grid.json.

Questions

Asked about this term

What is location-based grid carbon intensity?

The average carbon intensity of the electricity generated where the load runs, in gCO2e per kWh, before contracts or certificates. The index uses Ember's yearly figures (October 2026 release, 2024): Saudi Arabia 689, India 707, Norway 30, world 473.

What is the difference between location-based and market-based?

Location-based uses the average intensity of the grid where the electricity was consumed. Market-based uses the intensity of the supply you contracted, through certificates or power purchase agreements, and needs your own contract data. ESRS E1-6 asks for both; the index can supply only the first.

How much does location change the carbon of AI inference?

On the reference row, 23× between Saudi Arabia (43.7 kg CO2e per billion tokens) and Norway (1.9 kg), 2024 averages. The energy figure is identical in both places.

Can I file Ember's figure in my Scope 2 report?

Use it for comparison, not as the filed factor. It is a lifecycle, production-based figure on net generation, not a Scope 2 combustion factor, and it is an annual average that a round-the-clock GPU load does not follow hour by hour.

Where every figure came from

ML.ENERGY Leaderboard version 3.0 (released 1 December 2025; data compiled 16 February 2026; retrieved 26 September 2026), The ML.ENERGY Initiative, started at SymbioticLab, University of Michigan. Text Conversation task, 328 stable configurations. Energy per token is steady-state GPU energy divided by the output tokens generated in the window, measured with Zeus under vLLM 0.11.1 on H100 80GB HBM3 and B200 servers; energy per request is that figure times the average output length. Each index row is the lowest-energy configuration for its model and GPU at or under a 500 ms median inter-token latency, the leaderboard's own default ceiling, which no configuration reaches.
