Glossary

Energy per token

Energy per token is the GPU energy a serving system uses divided by the output tokens it generated in the same window, in joules per output token. The ML.ENERGY Leaderboard v3.0 measures it as steady-state GPU energy divided by the output tokens generated in the steady-state window, read from the device with Zeus under vLLM on H100 and B200 servers (data compiled 16 February 2026). On the index it runs from 0.047 J per token (Llama 3.1 8B Instruct, 1 × B200) to 2.06 J (Llama 3.1 405B Instruct FP8, 8 × H100); the index shows it as watt-hours per million output tokens.

The working

How the term is used on the index

Energy per token is average power divided by token throughput: on the reference row, 1,177.9 W summed across 2 cards ÷ 5,155.5 output tokens per second = 0.2285 J per token, which is 63.47 Wh/M. The identity is also the consistency check the RFP scoring box applies to a bidder's figure.

Three ways of getting the number are not equal. A measured figure reads the GPU's energy counter (NVIDIA NVML or DCGM, which Zeus uses) over a window of steady load and divides by the tokens served. A whole-system figure reads the server's power supplies and includes host, memory, network and fans, which on a DGX B200 adds about 1.79× to the GPU figure. An estimate multiplies a rated power (TDP) by an assumed utilisation and divides by an assumed throughput; it is the kind most often quoted and the kind the index does not publish. Energy per token falls as the running batch rises because the cards' fixed draw is spread over more tokens: see running batch.

Questions

Asked about this term

What is energy per token?

GPU energy divided by the output tokens generated in the same steady-state window, in joules per output token. The index measures it from the ML.ENERGY Leaderboard v3.0 and shows it as watt-hours per million output tokens; it runs from 0.047 to 2.06 J per token across the index rows.

How is energy per token measured?

By reading the GPU's energy counter (through NVML or DCGM; the leaderboard uses Zeus) at the start and end of a steady-state window under a fixed load, and dividing the difference by the output tokens generated in that window. The index's protocol asks for a window of at least 5 minutes after warm-up and at least 1,000 requests. Average power divided by tokens per second gives the same number.

Why do estimates of energy per token differ so much from measurements?

Estimates multiply a rated power by an assumed utilisation and divide by an assumed throughput, usually a single request at a time. A busy server serves hundreds of requests at once on nearly the same power: Qwen 3 32B on 2 × H100 moves 7.1× in energy per token between a running batch of 8 and 256. An estimate at one request per card lands far above every measured busy-server row.

Does energy per token include the host, the network and cooling?

Not on this index. The figures are GPU energy only. A whole-system reading adds the host, memory, network and fans (about 1.79× for a DGX B200, from NVIDIA's rated system and GPU power), and the facility's PUE adds cooling and distribution on top.
